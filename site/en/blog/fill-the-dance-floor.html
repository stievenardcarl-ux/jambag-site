How to fill a dance floor: the songs, the energy and a few secrets

After more than 300 concerts, we have a pretty clear idea of what gets a room on its feet. Spoiler: the songs matter a lot, but they are not everything.

It is not just about the songs

A dance floor fills up thanks to a combination of things. The songs play a big part, but so does the energy of the people playing them. A band that plays perfectly but without energy can completely miss the party.

You need a band that looks the part, knows how to work a crowd, sends out positive energy, and knows when to loosen people up. Many guests are dying to dance but don't dare go first. Our job is to make everyone feel at ease.

The secret few couples know

Guests follow the couple. If you are all in, the room will be too. And one small trick changes everything: brief your best men, bridesmaids and party-loving friends. Make them official mood-makers, and ask them to spend the night pulling people onto the dance floor. A full dance floor is a team effort.

Songs to sing, songs to dance

Not every popular song makes people dance. Some mostly make them sing: Seven Nation Army by The White Stripes, What's Up by 4 Non Blondes, Zombie by The Cranberries, or anything by Céline Dion bring the whole room together in chorus. Others get everyone moving even if they don't know the words. Our setlist is a careful mix of both.

Opening the party

Our signature setlist opens with familiar, upbeat, easy-to-follow songs to bring the first dancers in:

  1. I'll Be There for You, The Rembrandts
  2. Hey Ya!, OutKast
  3. Wake Me Up, Avicii
  4. Counting Stars, OneRepublic

Breathe, then go again

A party that is always flat out eventually wears people out. Sometimes you need a moment to breathe: a calmer song that lets dancers catch their breath without emptying the floor. For us, it is Hotel California by the Eagles. The whole room sings it, and we come back even stronger.

The finale

We save our best cards for the end:

  • Don't Stop Me Now, Queen
  • Freed from Desire, Gala
  • I Will Survive, Gloria Gaynor

Then comes a slow dance everyone waits for, ending with a wild guitar solo, and to close, September by Earth, Wind & Fire.

A touch of Paris

With French-speaking guests in the room, a few French classics work wonders: we have a whole French set, from Aznavour to the latest French pop hits.

The order matters as much as the songs

A good setlist is not just a list of hits. It alternates eras so every generation gets its moment, leaves room to breathe, and saves the best for last. That construction is what makes a party build instead of fading.

Our signature setlist is designed exactly that way. If some songs aren't for you, you can swap up to three. A fully bespoke show and special requests are also possible: they are simply added to the quote.
